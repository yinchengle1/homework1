03 · What happens in point addition?

Latest operation

Start at the identity point O

O + G = G

Rules used by the program

  • Identity: O + P = P.
  • Inverse: P + (−P) = O.
  • Different points: λ = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) mod p.
  • Doubling: λ = (3x₁² + a)/(2y₁) mod p.

Division means multiplying by a modular inverse, not ordinary decimal division.

Interpretation: coordinate frequencies and correlations describe this small deterministic group. They do not prove cryptographic security. The hash-function avalanche effect is a separate design goal and is not required of elliptic-curve scalar multiplication.

07 · What this demonstrates

  • Deterministic: the same k and G always produce the same Q.
  • Efficient forward direction: compute Q = kG using point addition or double-and-add.
  • ECDLP: on a properly chosen large curve, recovering k from G and Q is believed computationally infeasible.
  • Not a hash function: scalar multiplication has a group structure and is used for public-key cryptography and signatures.

08 · Limitations and Ethereum

  • This toy curve has only a small number of points; outputs repeat periodically.
  • Statistical appearance alone cannot establish one-wayness or security.
  • Ethereum uses secp256k1 with much larger parameters, not p = 17.
  • This demo does not generate real Ethereum keys or sign transactions.
